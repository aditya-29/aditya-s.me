# Researcher homepage comparison

Reviewed September 8, 2026.

## Scope: 113 distinct personal researcher pages

Inspected the retrieved homepage content and structure for each numbered reference below: identity and role placement, research descriptions, publications, professional links, and supporting sections. Twelve representative sites were also rendered in desktop Chrome and visually inspected. This is a design/content comparison, not an accessibility audit of those external sites or a claim to have read their research papers.

The sample is intentionally concentrated in machine learning, language models, vision, and robotics. It includes established faculty and industry researchers. Broken pages, access-denied pages, empty client-rendered shells, redirect-only pages, and generic institutional profiles are excluded from the numbered count. Website popularity or academic seniority does not imply that every design choice should be copied.

## Decisions for this website

- Keep the current role and research focus near the name. Use two short biography paragraphs.
- Put selected publications immediately after the introduction. Four papers do not need search, filters, pagination, or a carousel.
- Give each paper the same readable structure: venue/status, title, authors, short summary, and useful links. Keep Aditya highlighted within the correct author order.
- Use direct paper and PDF links where available, plus downloadable BibTeX citations. Keep a clear link to the full Scholar profile.
- Use restrained typography, a modest reading width, and one accent color. Remove the large decorative hero card and oversized marketing-style headline.
- Include concise career context. Never infer unpublished project ownership, numerical impact, exact employment dates, or an unverified degree.
- Keep the page usable without JavaScript, external font requests, animation, or an account.
- Defer a portrait, CV, and direct email until the actual assets/details are supplied. A working LinkedIn contact link is preferable to an invented address or empty button.

## Limitations

Design iteration: Aditya subsequently requested a different, more minimal layout with animated mathematical shapes. The page now uses a compact identity/navigation sidebar and sans-serif reading column. A native canvas supplies low-contrast, connectivity-preserving graph transformations in the margins. This adapts the compact/sidebar patterns in the original comparison; the 113-site research was not repeated. A pause control and reduced-motion support keep the animation optional.

Only twelve references received screenshot-based visual review. The larger comparison used retrieved HTML/content structure. References can change, move, or become unavailable. This audit does not verify the researchers’ current biographies. Publication completeness on Aditya’s page still depends on his Google Scholar export, because Scholar blocked retrieval. The exact CoreLLM LinkedIn description remains unavailable.
